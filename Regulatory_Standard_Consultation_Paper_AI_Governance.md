# Public Consultation Paper

**Reference ID:** REG-2030-CP-001
**Issuing Body:** The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision (AI Risk Taskforce)
**Date:** [Date, ~6 Months after HDL Activation]

**Subject: Proposed Regulatory Standard for the Governance of Artificial Intelligence Models in Global Systemically Important Financial Institutions (G-SIFIs)**

## 1. Introduction and Purpose

This paper invites public comment on a proposed new regulatory standard for the management and supervision of artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) models within Global Systemically Important Financial Institutions (G-SIFIs).

Over the past several years, this committee has closely monitored a multi-institutional initiative that developed a novel, three-tiered architecture for AI governance. This architecture, consisting of a local Supervisory Control Plane (SCP), a federated Governance Incident Exchange Network (GIEN), and a meta-analytical Hydra Defense Layer (HDL), has proven to be highly effective in managing both idiosyncratic and systemic AI risks in a live production environment. 

We have concluded that the principles underlying this architecture represent a new, higher standard of care. This proposal aims to codify these principles into a mandatory framework for all G-SIFIs to ensure the stability and integrity of the global financial system in an era of advanced AI.

## 2. The Three Pillars of the Proposed Framework

We propose that all G-SIFIs be required to implement systems that adhere to the following three pillars of AI governance:

*   **Pillar 1: Real-Time, Automated Local Supervision (The SCP Principle)**
    *   **Requirement:** All critical AI models must be governed by an automated system capable of real-time monitoring, policy enforcement, and intervention. This system must operate independently of the model it governs and be capable of taking automated actions (e.g., containment, shutdown) based on pre-defined, auditable policies.
    *   **Rationale:** Manual, periodic review is no longer sufficient to manage the risks posed by high-speed, autonomous models. Continuous, automated supervision is required.

*   **Pillar 2: Federated Threat Intelligence Sharing (The GIEN Principle)**
    *   **Requirement:** All G-SIFIs must participate in a mandatory, regulator-supervised network for the high-speed, anonymous sharing of AI threat intelligence. This includes, but is not limited to, intelligence on novel data drift, adversarial attacks, and model performance degradation.
    *   **Rationale:** Systemic risks require a systemic defense. No single institution can defend against threats that target the entire ecosystem. Collaborative, real-time intelligence sharing is a collective responsibility.

*   **Pillar 3: Proactive, Coordinated Defense (The HDL Principle)**
    *   **Requirement:** The federated network must be equipped with a meta-analytical capability to detect sophisticated, coordinated attacks against the network itself. The system must be able to identify improbable patterns of activity across institutions and defend against attempts to erode trust in the network.
    *   **Rationale:** As AI governance systems become critical infrastructure, they too will become targets. The defense network must be capable of defending itself against intelligent, state-level adversaries.

## 3. Request for Comment

The committee invites feedback from all stakeholders on the feasibility, scope, and implementation timeline for this proposed standard. The goal is to move from this consultation paper to a final binding regulation within 12 months.

The journey from a single sandbox experiment to this proposal has been remarkable. We believe that formalizing these proven principles into a global standard is the necessary and final step in securing the future of the international financial system.

**The age of AI requires a new architecture of trust. This is it.**
