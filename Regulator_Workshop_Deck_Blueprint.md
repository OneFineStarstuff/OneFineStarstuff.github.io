# Regulator Workshop Deck Blueprint (10 Slides)

**Topic:** The Future of AI Assurance: From Process Audits to Governance Integrity
**Audience:** Cross-functional group of financial supervisors (risk, IT, policy)
**Objective:** To facilitate a strategic discussion about the limitations of current AI assurance methods and the need for new supervisory paradigms like Governance-State Attestation.

---

**Slide 1: Title Slide**
*   **Title:** The Future of AI Assurance
*   **Subtitle:** A Workshop on Governance Integrity for the 2026–2035 Horizon
*   **Branding:** [Neutral - e.g., "Financial Stability Institute" or a similar body]

**Slide 2: Workshop Agenda (90 Minutes)**
*   Part 1: The AI Assurance Landscape Today (A Look Back)
*   Part 2: Introducing Governance Drift (The Core Challenge)
*   Part 3: Governance-State Attestation (A New Paradigm)
*   Part 4: Open Discussion & Future Outlook

**Slide 3: Where We Are Today: The "Process Assurance" Era (2020-2025)**
*   **Content:** Diagram showing the current supervisory model: an institution provides documentation (e.g., NIST RMF paperwork, model validation docs), and the supervisor audits the paperwork and processes.
*   **Key Message:** We currently audit the *promise* to be in control.

**Slide 4: The Emerging Gap: What Current Audits Miss**
*   **Content:** Simple list.
    *   Real-time model behavior
    *   Emergent properties in complex systems
    *   Misalignment between interacting AI agents
    *   The gap between documentation and reality ("Governance Drift")
*   **Key Message:** Process audits are static and backward-looking; they can't keep pace with dynamic AI systems.

**Slide 5: The Core Problem: "Governance Drift"**
*   **Content:** The same diagram from the executive presentation: "Approved Governance" vs. "Operational Governance."
*   **Key Message:** This is the central, shared challenge we must address.

**Slide 6: A New Paradigm: "Governance-State Attestation"**
*   **Content:** The 3-layer stack diagram (Policy, Model, Infrastructure) being summarized by a single "State Hash."
*   **Key Message:** We can move from auditing the *process* to verifying the *live state*.

**Slide 7: How It Works: A Simplified View**
*   **Content:** A simple 3-step flowchart.
    1.  **Institution:** Generates a daily cryptographic "Governance-State Hash."
    2.  **Submission:** The hash is submitted to the supervisor via an automated API.
    3.  **Supervisor:** Verifies the hash against a pre-registered "golden" hash. Any mismatch triggers an immediate inquiry.
*   **Key Message:** This is a move towards automated, continuous supervision.

**Slide 8: The Benefits of This Approach**
*   **Content:** Two columns.
    *   **For Supervisors:** Near real-time assurance, focus shifts from paperwork to anomalies, enables macro-prudential analysis of governance risk.
    *   **For Institutions:** Reduced manual audit burden, clear standard of care, fosters genuine trust and transparency.
*   **Key Message:** This is a win-win for safety and efficiency.

**Slide 9: Discussion Session: Key Questions**
*   *(This slide seeds the breakout discussion, covered in the facilitator guide)*
    1.  What are the biggest blind spots in our current AI supervisory methods?
    2.  Is "Governance Drift" a useful concept for framing the problem?
    3.  What are the practical and legal barriers to implementing a GSA-based approach?
    4.  How could this change the dynamic between supervisors and institutions?

**Slide 10: Next Steps & Closing Remarks**
*   **Content:** Summary of discussion themes, proposal for a follow-up working group, and thank you.
*   **Key Message:** This conversation is just the beginning.
