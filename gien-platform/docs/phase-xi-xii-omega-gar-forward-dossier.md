## <content>2. Phase XI — fictional multiversal governance framework</content>

### Governance objective
Phase XI (fictional epoch 2055–2065) models how a distributed governance system might maintain consistent control semantics across independent administrative domains without assuming that all domains share one authority, clock, infrastructure provider, or model implementation.

### Required architecture
1. **Constitutional rule registry:** authoritative versions, ratification history, dependency references and permitted transitions.
2. **Authority-envelope registry:** named principals and agents; permitted actions; scope; expiration; quorum requirements; revocation path.
3. **Cross-domain dependency map:** services, shared model providers, signing services, identity roots, ledgers and other systemic concentration points.
4. **Independent verification plane:** separate validation of policy digests, signatures, evidence roots, invariant results and package manifests.
5. **Conflict-resolution mechanism:** preserve competing claims, timestamps, dissent, jurisdiction boundaries and unresolved contradictions rather than silently normalizing them.
6. **Human/legal governance:** explicit decision owners, contestability, exception review and appeal or dispute processes appropriate to the fictional setting.
7. **Evidence portability:** documented schemas, canonical serialization, open export formats, independent readers and restoration tests.

### Phase XI daily metrics
 The report should include S_sys, G-SRI, C-SRI, CGR-I, CCR-I, A-SRI, P-SRI, Δ_drift and Δ_OSCAL only where their definitions remain appropriate and versioned. A Phase XI extension may add cross-domain divergence, invariant disagreement rate, stale-authority exposure, independent-verifier availability, evidence-replication coverage and recovery
duration. New metrics require
definitions, units, populations, normalization, missing-data rules, owners, calibrated thresholds and independent validation.

Do not use a single “multiversal coherence” percentage as a proxy for constitutional legitimacy. A mesh can be technically synchronized while acting outside its authority; conversely, legitimate disagreements can exist without a technical fault.

### Phase XI evidence acceptance
- No unresolved critical integrity failure in the active root/manifest chain.
- Authority changes must be traceable to authorized actors and configured quorum.
- Agent or automated action boundaries are independently enforced and tested.
- A control-plane partition does not permit stale or revoked authority to continue protected actions beyond the approved tolerance.
- Independently restored evidence is semantically interpretable and reproduces expected digests.
- Material model or policy changes have change review, regression test and rollback records.
- Negative results and counterexamples remain in the dossier, even after remediation.

## <content>3. Phase XII — Eternal Corpus preparation</content>

### Successor-epoch governance objective
Phase XII (fictional epoch 2065–2075) addresses archival persistence and interpretation across successor epochs, including long-lived data formats, cryptographic migration, independent custodianship, and recovery from partial loss. The Eternal Corpus is a fictional archive specification, not an existing planetary or interstellar system.

### Corpus contract
Define a canonical manifest with:
- corpus identifier and schema/version;
- enumerated artefact identifiers and content digests;
- media/format declaration and parser/reader specification;
- source epoch and creation time in UTC;
- signing algorithm, key identifier, trust-chain reference and signature digest;
- Merkle root and tree construction/version;
- provenance and classification label;
- retention/custodianship policy;
- privacy, consent, minimization, and permitted-use restrictions;
- redaction map and reason without inadvertently revealing removed content;
- restoration instructions and known limitations;
- migration history, including legacy algorithm/format deprecations;
- independent reviewer decision and last verified restore.

A corpus is not “survivable” merely because it has replicas. Measure administrative independence, provider diversity, geographic/environmental separation, credential independence, format diversity, restoration latency and corruption-detection capability.

### Beacon network design
The protocol should define at least three independent channels with separate operators, credentials, storage, monitoring and restoration procedures. For each channel, report:
- channel and custodian ID;
- topology/location at the granularity allowed by policy;
- source and destination key IDs;
- transport/authenticated-encryption profile;
- latency percentiles and failed transfer rate;
- last successful end-to-end restore;
- current root and verified root;
- signer/revocation validation;
- capacity/retention and recovery objective;
- administrative independence and known common-mode dependencies.

Three names for channels operated by the same identity provider, cloud account or root key do not establish three independent failure domains.

### Preservation and interpretation tests
1. Restore a sampled corpus from each channel using a dedicated recovery identity.
2. Recompute per-object digests and the corpus Merkle root.
3. Verify signatures against the versioned trust history, including revoked and expired keys.
4. Test readers across declared platform versions and documented open formats.
5. Reconstruct provenance and policy context, not merely bytes.
6. Test migration from deprecated algorithm identifiers and preservation of historical verification.
7. Demonstrate detection and safe quarantine of altered, missing, reordered or malformed artefacts.
8. Record whether restore was successful, partial, inconclusive or failed. Preserve all failure evidence.

## <content>4. Cryptographic lineage and recursive proof scaling</content>

### Cryptographic lineage
 Maintain a directed version graph from source artifact through canonical serialization, content digest, manifest, Merkle root, signature, encryption envelope, transport receipt, archive object version, and restoration verification. Each edge has an evidence record, time, implementation/tool version and actor. Signature validation must use a
maintained trust policy and
key-status history; a mathematically valid signature from an untrusted or revoked key does not establish an authorized submission.

### Algorithm transition
- ML-KEM under FIPS 203 is a key encapsulation mechanism for establishing shared secret material, used with a defined authenticated-encryption protocol.
- ML-DSA under FIPS 204 is a standardized digital signature scheme derived from CRYSTALS-Dilithium.
- SLH-DSA under FIPS 205 is a standardized hash-based digital signature scheme derived from SPHINCS+.
- Legacy RSA/ECC dependencies require inventory, migration prioritization and a verified hybrid/deprecation plan.
- Falcon/FN-DSA must only receive a production status consistent with the actual finalized standard, approved implementation, parameter set and deployment evidence at the time of reporting.

Do not assume an algorithm switch automatically secures old signed data. Define how long-lived signatures, timestamps, trust anchors, certificate status and old manifests remain verifiable after key rotation or algorithm retirement.

### Recursive proof scaling
 Where recursive aggregation is proposed, specify statement semantics, proof composition assumptions, verifier implementation, public-input binding, key rotation, circuit/program digests, batch membership, omitted-item detection, resource limits, denial-of-service controls and failure localization. Benchmark proof generation and verification across
realistic workload sizes. A
recursive proof that a collection of proofs verifies does not prove the underlying inputs were true or that all relevant requirements were included.

Move from Groth16 to a transparent proof system only after comparative evaluation of security assumptions, setup governance, proof size, latency, verifier cost, auditability and implementation maturity. Retain raw assessment evidence alongside compact proofs to prevent the proof from becoming the sole interpretation path.

## <content>5. Constitutional invariants and formal verification</content>

Candidate fictional CFE-1.0 / SGR-028 invariants include:
- **CI-001 Sovereignty Preservation:** no unilateral action dissolves an authority’s sovereignty without the specified valid treaty quorum.
- **CI-002 Cryptographic Continuity:** all accepted phase transitions preserve verifiable lineage for prior evidence.
- **CI-003 Agent Containment:** agents cannot exceed their delegated authority without the required human confirmation.
- **CI-004 Risk-Ceiling Response:** a sustained risk-ceiling breach causes a prescribed escalation, safe-mode decision and review.
- **CI-005 Quorum Integrity:** amendments require the defined quorum and authenticated voter/participant identities.
- **CI-006 Ledger Immutability:** accepted entries cannot be silently overwritten and any fork is explicitly reconciled.
- **CI-007 Fairness and Contestability:** impact-relevant decisions include reviewed fairness tests, explanations where required and an appeal path.
- **CI-008 Corpus Continuity:** the corpus is restorable through independent paths under the stated threat model.
- **CI-009 No-False-Seal:** an archive is not SEALED unless manifest, signature and retention prerequisites pass.
- **CI-010 Succession Authorization:** successor activation cannot proceed before explicit authorization and handoff review.

 For each invariant, preserve formal statement, variables, assumptions, environmental model, property class (safety/liveness), model checker/version, constants/bounds, run ID, result, counterexample digest, reviewer and implementation tests. A bounded check proves properties only relative to the declared model and assumptions. Unverified invariants
remain UNVERIFIED; they must
not be styled as VERIFIED because a snippet exists.

## <content>6. Systemic and existential risk treatment</content>

 S_sys, G-SRI, C-SRI, A-SRI and P-SRI can be used as fictional control-room indicators only after their data contract and calculation methods have been defined. p(doom) and p(doom)_kernel are especially sensitive to false precision: preserve assumptions, epistemic uncertainty and dissent; never represent them as scientific consensus or calibrated
real-world probabilities absent
substantiation.

The daily synthesis should distinguish:
- **observed operational risk:** sourced and timestamped events;
- **modelled scenario risk:** outputs conditioned on explicit scenarios and model assumptions;
- **epistemic uncertainty:** missing data, unknown mechanisms, disputed assumptions;
- **normative/constitutional risk:** possible violation of defined authority or rights;
- **existential speculation:** explicitly speculative scenarios that should not be mixed into measured incident statistics.

Recommended report shape: top drivers; scenario range; sensitivity to assumptions; evidence confidence; countervailing interpretation; intervention options; responsible decision owner; and trigger for reassessment. If a scenario cannot be estimated defensibly, mark it not quantified rather than fabricating a precise probability.

## <content>7. Phase XIII and Ω-GAR dissolution rehearsal</content>

### Phase XIII — pre-dissolution consolidation
The fictional Phase XIII epoch (2075–2081) is a controlled readiness phase. It must not presume that dissolution is inevitable or that current institutions have authority to authorize it. Required preconditions include:
- approved purpose, governance mandate and lawful authority within the fictional setting;
- inventory of unresolved risks and dissenting assessments;
- audit of all invariant changes and emergency exceptions;
- verified preservation manifests and independent restoration;
- documented successor-system capability and limits;
- explicit separation between archival transfer and transfer of governing authority;
- rollback/stop conditions for failed handoff;
- final independent review and dispute route.

### Ω-GAR dissolution arc
 Dissolution triggers may be classified as SCHEDULED, EMERGENCY or TREATY-MANDATED within the fictional model. Each trigger must carry authority, rationale, evidence, quorum, timestamp, constraints and challenge route. Emergency status must not silently waive cryptographic integrity or erase dissent; any allowed exception is explicit, time-limited
and independently reviewed.

### Successor activation
- **GIEN-UNIFIED-CODEX-OMEGA:** fictional successor specification, dependency manifest, compatibility requirements and known limitations.
- **GIEN-EPITAPH-OMEGA:** fictional closure declaration, archival scope and preservation commitments.
- **GIEN-INVOCATION-OMEGA:** fictional activation and handoff protocol.

Lifecycle states must be separate: DRAFT → REVIEWED → SEALED → TRANSMITTED → RESTORED/VERIFIED → AUTHORIZED FOR ACTIVATION → ACTIVATED. A successful signature or transfer does not itself confer legitimacy. If verification fails or quorum is not met, the transition remains blocked and existing evidence is preserved.

## <content>10. Supervisory interpretation and limitations</content>

 The philosophical purpose of a dissolution framework is to preserve accountability when a governance institution expects to change or cease operating. A memorial document is not a substitute for authority, verification or consent. A successor codex is not self-authorizing; transmission is not activation; archival continuity is not ethical
legitimacy. A sound framework preserves
the record of disagreement, acknowledges irreducible uncertainty, and keeps a fail-safe option when preconditions are unresolved.
